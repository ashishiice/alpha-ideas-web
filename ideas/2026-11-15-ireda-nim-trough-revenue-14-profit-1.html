🔍 Why This Works Now

1
The Q2 gap is the entire thesis, and it is four numbers wide. Revenue ₹2,345 crore, up 14%. Profit ₹554 crore, up 1%. Disbursements ₹10,579 crore, up 31%. Loan book ₹97,411 crore, up 15%. Read those together and the business is growing while the margin is not. IREDA lent 31% more money and earned 14% more interest on a book that grew 15% — but only 1% more profit, because what it pays to borrow repriced before what it charges to lend. That is arithmetic, not a judgment call. A lender whose funding cost rises 100bp while its loan yield rises 60bp loses 40bp of spread on a book that is 5.85x its net worth. It is also the single most reversible line item in the P&L the moment the policy rate stops moving.

The reversal has a date: December 4, 2026. The MPC meets December 2-4. The market assigns a 25bp hike a 77% probability, no change 13%, and 50bp or more just 8%. That distribution is not symmetric — it is a market saying "one more, then stop." Every serious forecaster is in the same place: Brickwork expects a final 25bp to 5.75% followed by "an extended pause," ICRA calls the December hike "almost certain," and SBI Research cut its call from 50bp to 25bp and sees terminal at 5.75-6.00%. When the repo stops at 5.75%, IREDA's incremental cost of funds plateaus within a quarter while the ₹97,411 crore book — 84% of it secured — keeps repricing upward. The spread turn is mechanical and it is one quarter away.
3
Growth is the part the peers do not have. PFC, the largest power financier in the country, saw disbursements fall 31.5% year-on-year to ₹34,131 crore while its loan book grew 2%. REC grew its book 5% sequentially but reported a 21% decline in profit and a 9% fall in net interest income. IREDA grew disbursements 31% and its book 15% in that same quarter, with a sanctions pipeline above ₹90,000 crore. India's 500 GW non-fossil target by 2030 is not a forecast that can be quietly dropped — it is the policy the entire renewable supply chain has been built against, and 100% of IREDA's book sits in that segment. You are not being asked to believe in a demand recovery. Demand never left.
4
Asset quality is moving the right way, which is what separates this from a value trap. Net NPA fell to 1.08% from 1.97% a year earlier. Provision coverage is 68.22%. CRAR is 20.30% against a 15% regulatory floor — the RBI's January 2026 risk-weight amendment for high-quality infrastructure projects cut IREDA's risk-weighted assets by ₹7,787 crore and added 183bp to its capital ratio in one stroke. CARE rates its bonds AAA; S&P rates it BBB globally. The gross NPA of 3.76% is high in absolute terms and is the honest counterpoint to the bull case — but it is falling, not rising, and the FY26 Gensol slippage is already provided for.
5
You are being paid to take the de-rating, not the growth. At ₹118.30 the stock trades at 2.32x book value of ₹51.0 and 16.9x trailing earnings. At its ₹152 high it traded near 3.0x. Nothing about the franchise got 23% worse — the loan book is bigger, the NPAs are lower and the capital ratio is stronger than when it printed that high. What changed is that the market repriced every lender for a terminal rate it now thinks is higher. That is a discount-rate move, and discount-rate moves reverse faster than earnings moves do.
6
This is a spread trade, so watch the spread — not the loan book. The single number that proves or kills the thesis is the gap between yield on term loans and cost of funds. FY26 yield was 9.5% against a cost of funds of 6.9%, down from 7.2% in FY25 — a 2.6-point spread. If the December pause holds and the spread re-widens toward 2.5-2.6% over the next two quarters, a 16% ROE business at 2.3x book is mispriced by 20-25%. If the spread compresses for a third consecutive quarter, the thesis is wrong and the ₹99 stop is the answer — not a lower average.

✗ Downside Risks

  • The deceleration may not be a spread trough. Revenue +14% with PAT +1% can persist if asset yields keep falling faster than funding costs — ICICI Direct flagged exactly this on PFC's book: "faster decline in asset yields relative to funding costs could sustain pressure on spreads."
  • A 50bp hike. Market-implied at 8%, but HSBC argued for it. That would take the 10-year through 7.50% and IREDA through ₹99, triggering the stop for a 16% loss.
  • 2.32x book is not cheap. If ROE slips below 13% and book growth falls under 12%, the multiple compresses to 1.9x — that is ₹97, not a recovery. There is no asset-value floor here the way there is at 0.8x book.
  • Gross NPA of 3.76% is genuinely high — four times PFC's 0.91%. One large renewable borrower slipping would undo two quarters of asset-quality improvement.
  • Capital-raise overhang. A 15%-growth lender at 5.85x gearing will need equity again; the FY26 QIP was ₹2,005.90 crore. Dilution at a depressed multiple is a real cost to the existing holder.
  • You are buying after a 15.6% eight-session run into a three-month high. Momentum cuts both ways, and the ₹98.78 low sits only 16% below the current price.
  • PSU discount and directed-lending risk. Tariff directives, mandated lending and leadership changes at a MNRE CPSE are not hedgeable and are not in the valuation.
