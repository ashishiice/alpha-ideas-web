1
NIM bottomed at 3.16%, and the culprit was FCNR(B), not the book. Q2 FY27 NIM on total assets fell 10bps QoQ to 3.16% (3.32% on interest-earning assets) — entirely the leverage cost of FCNR(B) deposits, with the core spread stable. Management guided to a positive NIM bias from Q3 FY27, citing the 7 October repo hike and a better retail/MSME mix (ICICI Direct, 19 October 2026). Even through that drag, NII grew 9.7% YoY to ₹34,619 crore and PAT rose 4.7% to ₹19,510 crore. A bank whose margin has stopped falling is a different instrument from the one the Street has been short for nine months.
2
A 50bp hike on 4 December is a NIM upgrade, not a cost — and that is not the consensus. About 70% of HDFC Bank's loans are floating-rate; deposits reprice with a two-quarter lag. Each 10bps of NIM on a ~₹45 lakh crore balance sheet is roughly ₹4,500 crore of annualised NII. A 50bp repo hike with a ~50% deposit beta adds 10-15bps of NIM by Q4 FY27 — ₹4,500-6,750 crore, or 5-8% of FY27E PAT of ₹81,657 crore, before a rupee of loan growth. SBI Research and Jefferies both expect 50bps on 4 December, Nomura 25bps. Either way, December is the peak of the cycle and the bank is on the right side of it.

HDFC Bank is the deepest FII vehicle in India at ₹2,300 crore of average daily traded value, and foreign money returning to India buys the largest, most liquid index weight first.
