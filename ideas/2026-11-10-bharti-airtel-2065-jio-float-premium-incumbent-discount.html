🔍 Why This Works Now

1
The Jio premium is a float artifact, not a valuation. Jio Platforms has a free float of about ₹28,500 crore — 2.2% to 2.5% of the company — because a ₹30,213 crore IPO sold only a sliver of a ₹13.3 lakh crore enterprise. What trades is a thin slice setting the price for the whole. Bharti Airtel, with a 50% free float, is priced by two-way institutional flow every single day. On identical arithmetic — market cap divided by annualised quarterly EBITDA — Jio is at 14.7x and Airtel at 9.1x. Jio's ₹12.9 lakh crore is the marginal clearing price of 2.2% of its shares; Airtel's ₹12.9 lakh crore is what the entire company clears at. That gap is not a growth premium anyone can underwrite — it is a scarcity price, and scarcity prices normalise when float expands.
2
Airtel out-earns Jio on every operating line, and grew EBITDA faster. Q2 FY27: revenue ₹60,878 crore vs ₹40,475 crore (+16.7% vs +11.4% YoY); EBITDA ₹35,240 crore vs ₹21,879 crore (+17.8% vs +16.6%); margin 57.9% vs 54.0%; PAT ₹8,887 crore vs ₹8,389 crore; ARPU ₹271 vs ₹218.8. Even India-alone revenue of ₹42,308 crore is larger than Jio's entire ₹40,475 crore. Jio's fair counter is that it converts more EBITDA into profit — 38% versus Airtel's 25% — because Airtel carries Africa, a heavier depreciation book and ₹88,277 crore of net debt. That conversion gap is the entire bear case, and it is precisely the variable now reversing.
3
The profit-conversion gap is closing from Airtel's side. Net debt fell ₹38,374 crore in twelve months to ₹88,277 crore — and a ₹17,900 crore dividend was paid in the same quarter out of free cash flow, not borrowings. Q2 free cash flow after interest was ₹12,100 crore, or ₹48,000 crore annualised: 3.7% of the market cap, with capex flat at ₹12,467 crore and management guiding core telecom capex to stay stable while incremental spend shifts to data centres. The drag is cash interest, which doubled to ₹6,200 crore as the spectrum moratorium ended — a peak, not a trend. ICICI's FY28E EPS of ₹80.5 sits 34% above FY27E, and the multiple on it is 26.2x.
4
The tariff repair is only half in the P&L. Airtel raised its entry 4G plan from ₹299 to ₹349 during the quarter, but ARPU rose only 2.7% sequentially — and part of even that was one extra day in the quarter. The full benefit lands in H2 FY27, and the December quarter is seasonally the strongest. Note what the street did: ICICI pushed its tariff-hike assumption out to FY28E, meaning the FY27 numbers do not need a price increase to work, and still lifted its target to ₹2,430. You are not paying for a hike; you are being given the flow-through of one already taken.
5
The ₹299 reversal is churn management, not a price war — which is why the stock is at ₹2,065. Net adds collapsed to 0.7 million against a ten-quarter average of 3.1 million, and churn rose to 2.9% from 2.6%. Reinstating a ₹299 entry plan is what an operator does when the bottom of the market is churning, not what it does before cutting prices across the book. Jio's ₹219 ARPU gives it 24% headroom to undercut Airtel — but Jio has just listed on a growth story, and a tariff war would destroy the very EBITDA narrative its new shareholders paid 14.7x for. Two listed telcos with a shared interest in ARPU repair is a better equilibrium than one private aggressor.
6
Valuation and positioning give you the entry. At ₹2,065 Airtel is 5% below its 52-week high of ₹2,175 while the Nifty is 12.7% above its October 8 low. It trades at 9.1x annualised EBITDA, 35.0x FY27E and 26.2x FY28E earnings, with a 29.8% FY28E ROE and a 1.4% dividend yield. Consensus target is ₹2,343; ICICI is at ₹2,430, Citi at ₹2,390 and Axis at ₹2,530. FIIs bought ₹7,212 crore of Indian equity on November 9 after ₹14,975 crore on November 5 — returning foreign money buys the largest, most liquid index weights first, and Airtel is one of them. You are buying a laggard in a rally, not a leader at the top.

⚖️ Risk-Reward Analysis

✓ Upside Factors

  • Tariff flow-through: a ₹50 ARPU increase across 377 million India mobile subscribers is ₹22,600 crore of annualised revenue; at the 67.7% incremental EBITDA margin that is roughly ₹15,300 crore, about 11% of FY27E EBITDA.
  • Deleveraging arithmetic: every ₹10,000 crore of net debt repaid at 8% saves ₹800 crore of pre-tax interest, roughly ₹600 crore of PAT — 1.7% of FY27E profit per tranche, and net debt has already fallen ₹38,374 crore in a year.
  • Multiple gap: 12x annualised EBITDA — still below Jio's 14.7x — is ₹2,710 per share, 31% above Monday's close.
  • Data centres and enterprise: Nxtra's 1 GWh build-out and Airtel Business growing EBITDA 15% give the SoTP optionality that a pure-mobile multiple ignores.
  • Africa: revenue grew 6.3% QoQ in dollar terms, with constant-currency EBITDA growth running ahead of India.
  • Flow: FII buying has resumed, Airtel is a top-five Nifty weight, and it is the most liquid way to own Indian telecom without a 2.2% float.

✗ Downside Risks

  • Jio price war. Jio's ₹219 ARPU gives it 24% headroom under Airtel's ₹271. A nationwide 10-15% data tariff cut hits net adds and ARPU at once — and Airtel's own ₹299 reversal proves it will defend share before it protects ARPU.
  • Churn is already real: 0.7 million net adds against a 3.1 million ten-quarter average, churn 2.9% versus 2.6%. Two more quarters below 1.5 million net adds and the premiumisation story stalls.
  • Cash interest doubled to ₹6,200 crore from ₹2,600 crore as the spectrum moratorium ended. If dues keep escalating, free cash flow after interest compresses even while EBITDA grows.
  • Macro event risk: October CPI above 6.1% on November 12 plus a hawkish MPC on December 3, into a market already up 12.7% in a month. A 5-8% index correction takes a high-beta large cap down 8-12%.
  • Global: the US 10-year at 4.876% on strong payrolls. A further rise pulls money out of emerging markets, and a rupee at 94.47 is still weak enough to make that easy.
  • Regulatory: an adverse AGR ruling, a new licence-fee interpretation, or a TRAI consultation that caps tariffs. Indian telecom risk is regulatory before it is competitive.
  • Valuation: 35x FY27E is not statistically cheap. If FY28 EPS slips from ₹80.5 to ₹70, the same 26x multiple is ₹1,820 — below the entry.
