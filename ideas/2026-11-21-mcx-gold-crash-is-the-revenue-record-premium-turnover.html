🔍 Why This Works Now

1
Revenue is a fee on volatility, not on direction. MCX charges an ad-valorem transaction fee on options premium. Q2 FY27 options premium ADT was ₹10,291 crore a day against ₹12,00,129 crore of notional — a 0.86% premium-to-notional ratio, and bullion has held a stable 0.37% through the whole cycle. The exchange gets paid more when prices move and it does not care which way. Gold fell from $5,595/oz in January to $3,527; silver from $121.63 to $41.12. A crash of that violence is not a quiet tape, and MCX is the toll booth on it.
2
October already beat the Q3 consensus; November was worse. MCX handled ₹363.9 trillion of notional volume in October, up 20% month-on-month and 139% year-on-year. Options premium ADTO hit a record ₹12,100 crore a day — flat on September but 17.6% above the Q2 average of ₹10,291 crore, and explicitly above Jefferies' and consensus Q3 FY27 estimates. November then delivered gold down 12% in eleven sessions and silver down 19%, i.e. higher realised volatility than October. Q3 prints in early February with the run-rate already banked.
3
Operating leverage is extreme: a 78% EBITDA margin and 53% PAT margin. Q2 FY27 revenue rose 113.9% year-on-year to ₹800 crore and EBITDA rose 145.9% to ₹664 crore — a 78.0% margin, up 1,070bps. PAT more than doubled to ₹455 crore, and H1 FY27 PAT of ₹868 crore is already 65% of the whole of FY26. Because roughly 55% of operating cost is variable and regulatory, incremental revenue falls almost straight through: every extra ₹100 crore of revenue is worth about ₹78 crore of EBITDA and ₹53 crore of PAT.
4
The multiple is high — but the earnings base is being revised up faster than the multiple. ICICI Securities models FY27E EPS of ₹70 and FY28E of ₹85, a 21% step-up; Motilal Oswal puts the FY26-28 PAT CAGR at 27%. At ₹3,535 that is 50.5x FY27E and 41.6x FY28E. Jefferies values the stock at 45x December-2028 EPS for ₹4,300 (bull ₹4,730 at 50x, bear ₹3,130 at 35x); ICICI at 46x FY28E for ₹3,900; UBS at ₹4,500. The risk is a de-rating, not a deceleration — which is why this idea carries a hard stop and a position cap rather than a buy-and-forget label.
5
The growth is more structural than the bears allow. Traded clients rose 89.8% year-on-year to 14.98 lakh in Q2, and H1's 19.75 lakh is already close to FY26's full-year 20.9 lakh. FPI count rose from about 220 to 250. Base-metal F&O ADT grew 681% to ₹20,605 crore, electricity futures hold roughly 75% share, silver 100g and gold 10g futures are scaling, and far-dated contracts now run to 12-18 months. Jefferies attributes only 25-35% of the FY27 options-premium increase to volatility — the other 65-75% is structural.

✗ Downside Risks

  • Volatility normalisation is the entire risk. If gold settles into a 5% range in Q4 FY27, premium ADT could fall back to ₹8,000-9,000 crore and FY28E EPS of ₹85 becomes roughly ₹65 — at 41x that is ₹2,665, a 25% drawdown from spot.
  • Valuation: 50.5x FY27E and 41.6x FY28E for a regulated toll road. Jefferies' own bear case of ₹3,130 sits below the entry price.
  • Premium realisation is compressing: 1.11% in Q2 FY26, 0.92% in Q1 FY27, 0.86% in Q2 FY27, 0.72% in October. Notional can grow while revenue falls — that is the specific failure mode to watch.
  • Mix leakage: gold futures volume hit a 21-month low in October and Indian gold ETFs have taken five consecutive months of net inflows, pulling retail flow away from the futures complex.
  • Regulatory and competitive: SEBI has already changed this business twice (SGF rationalisation in August 2026, contract-tenure and expiry rules), and the exchange itself flags rising competitive intensity despite a 98% share.
