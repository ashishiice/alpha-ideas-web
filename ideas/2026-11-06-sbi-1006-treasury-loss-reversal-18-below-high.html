🔍 Why This Works Now

1
The Q2 miss was three line items, and two of them reverse in Q3. Headline PAT of ₹23,038 crore was flattered by a ₹7,288 crore exceptional gain on the 6.35% SBI Funds Management stake sale, which listed during the quarter. Strip it out and core PAT was roughly ₹17,000 crore, about 15% below Antique's estimate. The gap was almost entirely (a) a ₹1,277 crore treasury loss against a ₹2,877 crore gain in Q1 — a ₹4,154 crore sequential swing — and (b) PSLC and business-acquisition costs deliberately pulled forward into H1. Neither is a credit event. NII rose 14.7% to ₹49,143 crore, domestic NIM printed 3.11%, GNPA fell to 1.39% — the lowest in more than two decades — and provisions dropped 33%.
2
The yield that caused the loss has already retraced a third of the move. SBI's domestic investment book is ₹17.86 lakh crore, of which 72% sits in HTM and roughly 28% — about ₹5 lakh crore — is AFS/HFT and marked to market. The benchmark 10-year went from 7.50% in late October to 7.17% at Thursday's close and 7.10% this morning: a 33-40 basis point rally in six sessions, driven by the crude collapse and the Democratic sweep of Congress. At a modified duration of three, 25 basis points on ₹5 lakh crore is roughly ₹3,750 crore of pre-tax mark-up — more than reversing Q2's loss. That is an estimate, not a disclosure, but the direction and the order of magnitude are not in doubt. Q3, reported in early February, is where it prints.
3
The street cut treasury assumptions at exactly the wrong moment. Antique raised NII estimates 3% for FY27/28 to factor in 50 basis points of rate hikes, but simultaneously cut other income 'on lower treasury gains' and trimmed its target to ₹1,155 from ₹1,255. Nuvama cut earnings 2-6% and its target to ₹1,225. That is the mechanical, post-hoc response to a single bad quarter. Consensus FY27 other income now embeds a treasury drag that the bond market has already unwound. An upgrade cycle into Q3 is the most likely positive surprise in Indian large-cap banking.
4
Both directions of the rate trade help SBI — which is why the 'hikes hurt banks' framing is wrong here. SBI's asset book is overwhelmingly floating-rate and EBLR-linked, so a repo hike reprices loans within a quarter while deposits reprice with a lag. Citi said precisely this after Q2: rising rates plus a high floating-rate mix support margin expansion. So a continued hike cycle helps NII, which is already in consensus, while a pause-plus-bond-rally helps treasury, which is not. The bear case needs yields rising and NIM compressing at the same time — that needs a crude shock, not a rate decision.
5
Valuation is undemanding, but be honest about the source of return: this is book-value compounding, not a re-rating. SBI trades at ₹1,006 against September book value of ₹728 — 1.38x book, 10.9x trailing earnings, a 1.72% dividend yield and 15.4% ROE. That multiple is near the top of its own ten-year range, so do not underwrite a PSU re-rating. The return comes from book growth: 15.4% ROE on a ~19% payout compounds book value at about 12.5%, putting September-2027 book near ₹819. At 1.45x — only 0.07x of expansion — that is ₹1,188, plus roughly ₹17 of dividends: about 19.5% total return. The street's average target is ₹1,232 across 49 analysts with not a single sell; CLSA has ₹1,325 and Emkay ₹1,350. My target sits below consensus on purpose.
