🔍 Why This Works Now

The RBI's own auction tape is the tell, and almost nobody read it. On 21 October the Reserve Bank walked away from the entire ₹23,000 crore T-bill programme rather than accept the market's price: 91-day bids ₹15,366.60 crore against ₹8,000 crore notified (1.9x), 182-day ₹18,925.50 crore against ₹8,000 crore (2.4x), 364-day ₹11,352.50 crore against ₹7,000 crore (1.6x) — every competitive bid rejected, and every non-competitive bid rejected too, including ₹12,028.76 crore of 91-day retail money (total retail ₹15,425 crore). The prior week's cut-offs were 5.73% / 6.29% / 6.49%; the Reuters poll had forecast 5.85% / 6.42% / 6.60%. The RBI refused to pay it. That 364-day 6.49% was 99bp over a 5.50% repo — a four-year high — after the three cut-offs had already jumped 64bp / 83bp / 69bp in the five weeks to 14 October. A sovereign that rations its own short-term paper at 1.6-2.4x cover is telling you something about where it thinks short rates should be.

2

The 198bp term premium is being fought with quantity tools, not the repo. Look at what the RBI has actually deployed since the 7 October hike: a 20% cash reserve on forward dollar purchases above $2 million (FX Risk Reserve), a special dollar window for the public-sector oil marketing companies, the daily minimum CRR maintenance raised to 99% from 90% (a liquidity drain without touching the CRR rate), a ₹25,000 crore OMO sale on 13 October, and simultaneously a 4-day Variable Rate Repo on 26 October. That is the 2013 taper-tantrum playbook: defend the currency with reserves, liquidity plumbing and FX curbs so you do not have to defend it with the policy rate. In that playbook the 10-year's peak arrives before the last hike, because the market prices the rate path while the RBI is still managing the currency.

  • FOMC pauses on 28 October and the September Fed hike proves terminal: India's 10-year typically follows US 10-year moves with a 0.5-0.7 beta.
  • A dovish MPC surprise on 2-4 December — 25bp and a shift out of "calibrated tightening" — is worth 30-50bp on the 10-year in a session.
  • Supply relief: FY27 gross borrowing is already budgeted. If the RBI restores OMO purchases in H2 FY27, it absorbs the ₹34,000 crore/week of 10-year supply that is currently sitting on the market.
  • Tax shape: 12.5% LTCG on the ₹3.60 discount-to-par versus 30% slab on FD interest = ~198bp of after-tax edge at a 30% slab.

✗ Downside Risks

  • Brent to $130+ (the 52-week high is already $126.10): CPI to 6.2-6.5%, a 50bp December hike plus 25bp in February, 10-year to 7.95-8.10% = -3.2% to -4.1% of price.
  • Rupee through ₹97: forces a harder liquidity drain and possibly a repo response; short rates spike, the curve steepens and the 10-year is dragged to 7.75-7.90% even without a hike.
  • Unabsorbed supply: ₹34,000 crore of 10-year paper every week with no OMO bid can push the term premium to 7.9-8.0% on technicals alone.
  • Hawkish FOMC on 28 October plus another Fed hike: EM debt outflows, India 10-year +20-25bp in a week.
  • Fiscal slippage: oil subsidies plus higher interest cost. Watch the H2 FY27 borrowing calendar in late January — an expansion is the cleanest bear catalyst.
  • The rejection could mean the opposite of my read: if the RBI skipped the T-bill auctions because it intends to keep the system tight through other instruments, short rates stay elevated and the 10-year grinds up rather than down. This is why the entry is laddered and the stop is hard.
  • Mark-to-market is real: a 50bp back-up is -3.4% before carry. Over six months the 7.47% coupon covers it; over six weeks it does not.

Risk Mitigation

  • Cap the duration at 25-30% of the debt allocation (max ~15% of the total portfolio). The balance sits in 364-day T-bills at 6.49% or 1-year FDs at 6.50-6.75% — zero mark-to-market — so a 50bp back-up costs the blended book ~1%, not 3.4%.

  • 7.85% is the last tranche, not a new opportunity. No averaging down below it — that rule is what separates a position from a grudge.
  • Time stop as well as price stop: if the 10-year has not touched 7.30% by 30 June 2027, sell half and reassess. A flat 12 months is a failed thesis even with a 7.5% coupon.
  • No leverage, no borrowing, no selling equity at a loss to fund this. The edge is the coupon, not the gearing.
  • Switch rule: if the 5s10s steepens beyond 40bp, move the position to the 5-year (6.53% GS 2031) — that is where the hike premium is actually stored.
  • Event gate: a 50bp hike with a hawkish stance on 4 December means exit at market on 5 December, before the February MPC prices in more.
