🔍 Why This Works Now

1
The second hike already happened — in the call market, not at the MPC. The RBI raised repo 25bp on 7 October. Since 14 October the weighted average call rate has run at 5.70%, 20bp above the new repo, because the RBI under-supplied five straight variable-rate repo auctions (on 26 October it offered ₹1,50,000 crore against ₹1,86,000 crore of bids). Latha Venkatesh's read in Moneycontrol on 27 October was right: that is a second 25bp hike, delivered through liquidity. A liquidity-driven tightening reverses far faster than a rate-driven one, because the central bank can undo it in a single auction rather than waiting for the next policy meeting.
2
The credit curve has inverted against the sovereign curve, and that is not a credit signal. On 27 October a 1-year AAA bank CD printed 8.10% (from 7.50% on 14 October) and a 1-year NBFC commercial paper 8.40% (from 7.85%). A one-year bank liability now yields 78bp more than a five-year sovereign. Bank and NBFC credit has not deteriorated by 55-60bp in thirteen days — the funding market has. When a one-year CD pays more than a five-year G-Sec, the market is charging you for cash, not for risk, and cash panics are the most reliably temporary thing in a bond market.
3
The 2-year moved three times as far as the OIS market — that arithmetic does not close. The 2-year G-Sec went from 6.22% on 28 September to 7.00% on 28 October and 7.12% by 30 October, a 90bp repricing. Over the same fortnight the 1-year OIS moved only 31bp (6.35% to 6.66%) and the 6-month OIS 36bp. If this were a genuine repricing of the policy path, the OIS curve — the purest read on expected repo — would have moved with it. It did not. The 46bp gap between the 2-year bond and the 1-year OIS is a liquidity and balance-sheet premium, and it is the part of the yield that mean-reverts without the RBI cutting a single rate.
4
You are being paid 22bp to take 2.4 extra years of duration. Five-year 7.32%, ten-year 7.55%. The 5s10s spread is 23bp and the 2s10s just 43bp — a curve with slope on a chart and almost no compensation in the hand. Per unit of duration the belly pays roughly twice what the long end does: 7.33% for 2.9 years against 7.55% for 6.8 years. In a market where a $115 Brent print can add 15bp in a session, take the carry and refuse the convexity bill.
5
The breakeven is the trade. Three months of carry on the ladder is 1.83%. At a blended duration of 2.9 that means yields can rise 63bp and you still break even; on the ten-year, a 28bp selloff wipes out a full quarter of carry. Over twelve months you would need yields roughly 290bp higher to lose money. That is the whole idea — not a forecast that yields fall, but a structure in which being wrong for another quarter costs almost nothing while the squeeze unwinds.
