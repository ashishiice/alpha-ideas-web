The trough is already in the base. A refiner printing 1.5% OPM earned 12% OPM in Mar-2026 and 13% in Sep-2023; that is the range this business reverts to when crude and the rupee stop fighting it.

Four tailwinds landed after the quarter closed. (a) Effective 16 October the diesel export duty was cut to ₹8.5/litre from ₹25 six weeks earlier, while the petrol levy rose only to ₹2.5 — a net transfer to IOC's export barrel that does not appear in the Q2 numbers at all. (c) Brent fell 5.1% in three sessions, from $116.72 on 23 October to $110.78 today, on Tehran talks involving Saudi Arabia, Turkey, Pakistan and Iran. ✗ Downside Risks

  • Q2 can be worse than Q1. July-September carried the highest Brent of the conflict, and IOC holds roughly 60 days of inventory — a falling crude price creates inventory losses in the quarter it falls, even as it helps the next one.
  • Policy risk is the real risk. New Delhi can cut retail petrol and diesel prices to pass on cheaper crude, capping the marketing margin exactly when it recovers. IOC's earnings are a policy derivative, not a pure commodity derivative.
  • A failed Tehran round or a Hormuz disruption takes Brent back above $125 and IOC to ₹110-115.
  • A hawkish Fed tonight pushes USDINR back toward 96-97 and widens working-capital losses.
  • The H1 FY27 loss may force a dividend cut — the 6.3% yield is trailing, not contractual.
  • Global refining overcapacity keeps GRMs at $5-6/bbl; there is no refining boom coming to rescue the marketing book.
