Petcoke and diesel are cement's two largest variable costs and both lag crude by one to two quarters - which puts the relief exactly in the December-to-March window that this position is designed to own.

Meanwhile capacity goes from 109 MTPA today (including Sanghi, Penna and Orient) to 119 MTPA by March 2027, with the group's stated target revised up to 140-155 MTPA by FY28. The 90% profit fall is a tax artefact, not an operating collapse. Q2 FY26 carried a ₹1,697 crore income-tax provision reversal that inflated that quarter's base. Q2 FY27 revenue still rose 10% to ₹6,113 crore and EBITDA per tonne rose sequentially. The trailing P/E of 32.6 and ROE of 8.85% are therefore measuring a depressed denominator, not a broken business.

  • Capacity of 119 MTPA by March 2027 with FY26 volume growth of 14.7% - operating leverage on a fixed cost base.
  • Three analysts at ₹470 / ₹600 / ₹680 - wide dispersion because the earnings base is distorted, not because the asset is in dispute.
  • 12.6x forward EV/EBITDA reverting to the 15.2x five-year average is a re-rating on its own, before a single extra tonne is sold.

✗ Downside Risks

  • Adani group governance and related-party risk. Part of the discount is structural and may never close - this is the single largest reason the position is capped at 8%.
  • A cement price war. If the December-March hikes reverse and trade price falls below ₹245 per bag, the entire margin thesis breaks and the stop must be honoured without argument.
  • Brent re-spiking above $105 on a Strait of Hormuz reversal would remove the cost-relief leg in one move.
  • Rates to 6.00% and beyond slowing housing and infrastructure demand into FY28 - cement volumes are the first casualty of a housing slowdown.
  • A ₹14,500 crore capex programme that dilutes returns if volumes disappoint; the group has a history of announcing capacity faster than it commissions it.
  • Trailing ROE of 8.85% and a 0.50% dividend yield leave no accounting or income cushion while you wait.
