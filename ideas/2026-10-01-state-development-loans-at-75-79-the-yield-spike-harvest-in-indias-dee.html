2. SDL spread over G-Sec is 60-90 bps wide

Per CCIL tenor-wise yields, Maharashtra SDLs 2031 traded at ~7.09% vs the equivalent-maturity G-Sec benchmark — spreads on longer SDLs (2032-2033) are running 75-90 bps. State debt carries an explicit government guarantee with zero downgrade history — you're paid for liquidity, not credit risk.

3. RBI liquidity tightening is the peak of the pain cycle

The RBI has been draining surplus liquidity to defend the rupee and anchor inflation. Historically, once the drain matures, yields mean-revert or the RBI pivots to injecting — both push bond prices up sharply from this base.

4. State borrowing supply is front-loaded and absorbed

H2 FY27 state borrowing programs are heavy (₹4.5-5 lakh crore), but the RBI's primary-dealer underwriting and continued FPI access ensure supply meets demand. Heavy supply = cheap entry prices, not perpetual rising yields.

5. Retail Direct gives zero-cost, direct access

Buying SDLs via RBI Retail Direct costs nothing (no brokerage, no platform fee, no exit load) and delivers coupon straight to your bank account — unlike gilt funds which layer on 0.2-0.8% in fees before returning performance.
